# MASTERED LANGUAGE COACH
## Hindi → English | Mission 01: My First Introduction

### Mission outcome

The learner can confidently introduce themselves in English for 45–60 seconds using their name, place, role, interests, and a polite closing.

### Psychological learning design

This first mission is intentionally low-pressure. Start with recognition and repetition, then move through small successful speaking steps: phrase → personal sentence → guided introduction → independent introduction. Correct only the highest-impact errors. Celebrate completion, not accent perfection. The learner should leave believing: “I can start speaking English now.”

### Core English phrases

| English | Hindi meaning | Pronunciation guide |
|---|---|---|
| Hello. | नमस्ते। | हेलो |
| My name is ___. | मेरा नाम ___ है। | माय नेम इज़ ___ |
| I am from ___. | मैं ___ से हूँ। | आइ ऐम फ्रॉम ___ |
| I live in ___. | मैं ___ में रहता/रहती हूँ। | आइ लिव इन ___ |
| I am a ___. | मैं एक ___ हूँ। | आइ ऐम अ ___ |
| I enjoy ___. | मुझे ___ करना पसंद है। | आइ एंजॉय ___ |
| It is nice to meet you. | आपसे मिलकर अच्छा लगा। | इट इज़ नाइस टु मीट यू |

### Key usage notes

- Use **My name is** for a name, not *Myself…*.
- Use **I am from** for your hometown, state, or country.
- Use **I live in** for where you live now.
- Keep the first introduction clear and simple. Short correct sentences are better than a long memorized speech.

### Model introduction

Hello. My name is Riya Sharma. I am from Lucknow, India. I live in Delhi. I am a college student. I enjoy reading and listening to music. It is nice to meet you.

### Model dialogue

**A:** Hello. My name is Riya Sharma. I am from Lucknow. I am a college student. It is nice to meet you.

**B:** Hello, Riya. It is nice to meet you too. What do you enjoy doing?

**A:** I enjoy reading and listening to music.

### 6–8 minute video lesson brief

Create a detailed, supportive lesson for Hindi-speaking beginners. Narrate mainly in Hindi while displaying and modelling the English phrases. Structure the lesson as:

1. 0:00–0:45 — reduce speaking fear; explain that clarity matters more than perfection.
2. 0:45–2:15 — teach name, place, and current city with Hindi meanings and slow pronunciation.
3. 2:15–3:30 — teach role and interest phrases; correct the common error “Myself…” gently.
4. 3:30–4:45 — build a full introduction one sentence at a time.
5. 4:45–6:15 — model the dialogue with captions; explain how to answer “Nice to meet you.”
6. 6:15–7:30 — give a confidence challenge: speak for 45–60 seconds using five details.

Use a calm tone, meaningful repetition, on-screen English text, Hindi explanation, natural Indian learner examples, and no unnecessary grammar terminology.

### 15–18 minute guided audio practice brief

Create an immersive Hindi-guided practice session, not a casual discussion. Include:

1. A one-minute confidence warm-up and breathing pause.
2. Slow listen-and-repeat practice for every phrase, with 5–7 second learner pauses.
3. Pronunciation focus for “name,” “from,” “live,” “enjoy,” and “nice.”
4. Substitution drills: change name, place, role, and interest.
5. Call-and-response dialogue practice.
6. A 45-second silent planning pause.
7. Two full guided self-introduction attempts, then one independent attempt.
8. A calm summary and instruction to record the final version.

Narrate primarily in Hindi, retain all practice phrases in English, and give enough silence for real learner responses.

### AI role-play prompt

You are a friendly English conversation partner for a beginner Hindi speaker. Begin in simple English: “Hello! Nice to meet you. Please introduce yourself.” Let the learner finish before correcting. First praise what was clear. Then give no more than three useful corrections in Hindi across fluency, vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation, response quality, and confidence. Ask for a retry. Keep the English simple. Do not shame accents or demand perfection.

### Completion rule

The learner completes Mission 01 after delivering a 45–60 second introduction with at least five personal details: name, place, current city, role, and interest; and responding politely to “Nice to meet you.”

### Human quality review rule

A proficient Hindi–English educator must check the Hindi wording, pronunciation guidance, generated script, audio clarity, video captions, and cultural naturalness before release.
